## 3. The directed program and how its expectations were derived

The program is a literal byte stream at `0x0100` (96 bytes). Each encoding is
taken from the instruction figures in A22-6821-7; the expected architectural
state after the program is derived by hand from the descriptions on the cited
pages, not from the RTL.

| Addr | Bytes | Instruction | Effect (manual rule) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0100 | `41 10 02 00` | `LA 1,0x200` | R1 = 0x200 (p. 65) |
| 0104 | `41 20 02 04` | `LA 2,0x204` | R2 = 0x204 |
| 0108 | `58 30 10 00` | `L 3,0(1)` | R3 = 7 (p. 26) |
| 010C | `5A 30 20 00` | `A 3,0(2)` | R3 = 12, CC 2 (p. 27) |
| 0110 | `50 30 10 08` | `ST 3,8(1)` | storage[0x208] = 12 |
| 0114 | `59 30 10 08` | `C 3,8(1)` | equal, CC 0 (p. 30) |
| 0118 | `47 80 01 28` | `BC 8,0x128` | mask 8 = CC 0, taken (p. 74) |
| 011C | `41 50 0E AD` | `LA 5,0xEAD` | skipped |
| 0120 | `41 50 0B AD` | `LA 5,0xBAD` | skipped |
| 0124 | `41 60 0A BC` | `LA 6,0xABC` | skipped |
| 0128 | `92 40 10 0C` | `MVI 12(1),0x40` | byte at 0x20C = 0x40 (p. 53) |
| 012C | `95 40 10 0C` | `CLI 12(1),0x40` | equal, CC 0 (p. 54) |
| 0130 | `12 33` | `LTR 3,3` | positive, CC 2 (p. 27) |
| 0132 | `47 20 01 3A` | `BC 2,0x13A` | mask 2 = CC 2, taken |
| 0136 | `41 50 0B AD` | `LA 5,0xBAD` | skipped |
| 013A | `54 30 20 00` | `N 3,0(2)` | 12 AND 5 = 4, CC 1 |
| 013E | `56 30 20 00` | `O 3,0(2)` | 4 OR 5 = 5, CC 1 |
| 0142 | `57 30 20 00` | `X 3,0(2)` | 5 XOR 5 = 0, CC 0 |
| 0146 | `50 30 10 10` | `ST 3,16(1)` | storage[0x210] = 0 |
| 014A | `58 40 10 14` | `L 4,20(1)` | R4 = 0x7FFFFFFF |
| 014E | `5A 40 10 18` | `A 4,24(1)` | R4 = 0x80000000, overflow, CC 3 |
| 0152 | `1B 44` | `SR 4,4` | R4 = 0, CC 0 |
| 0154 | `55 30 10 00` | `CL 3,0(1)` | 0 vs 7, CC 1 (p. 55) |
| 0158 | `18 63` | `LR 6,3` | R6 = 0, CC unchanged |
| 015A | `41 F0 01 5E` | `LA 15,0x15E` | R15 = 0x15E |
| 015E | `07 FF` | `BCR 15,15` | branch to itself (p. 74) |

Expected final state, derived from the table above before running anything:

- R1 = 0x200, R2 = 0x204, R3 = 0, R4 = 0, R5 = 0 (both `LA 5` skipped),
  R6 = 0, R15 = 0x15E;
- CC = 1 (set by `CL` at 0x154; `LR`, `LA` and `BCR` do not change it);
- ILC = 1 for the final two-byte `BCR`;
- storage: word 12 at 0x208, byte 0x40 at 0x20C, word 0 at 0x210;
- fixed-point overflow recorded by the `A` at 0x14E.

### 4.4 Defects found by the checks

Two real defects were found and fixed during this increment. Both are recorded
because a check that has never failed is not evidence.

1. **Two-byte fetch (found by simulation).** The first fetch loop decoded a
   two-byte RR instruction after fetching only the opcode byte, so `LTR 3,3`
   executed with a stale second byte, the branch at `0x132` was not taken, and
   the run ended in the watchdog with
   `FAIL trace[11]: retired ia=000136, expected 00013a`. The loop now always
   fetches the full length derived from the opcode's format bits.
2. **Spurious fixed-point overflow (found by a formal cover).** `ovf_pending`
   was latched from the adder's overflow output on every instruction, and `LR`
   drives the adder as a pass-through, so an `LR` between two operands of equal
   sign could raise a false overflow flag. The latch is now qualified by the
   instruction's "sets condition code" property. The directed program's
   overflow check still passes, and the core cover set no longer claims an
   overflow trace that one instruction cannot reach from reset.

## 5. Limits and deviations

The list in [../spec/spec.md](../spec/spec.md) §5 is the authority. In summary:

- no interruptions, so fixed-point overflow sets CC 3 and a sticky flag
  instead of taking a program interruption, and an unknown opcode halts
  instead of taking an operation exception;
- no specification (alignment) exceptions; operands must be aligned;
- no protection, no DAT, no wait or problem state;
- no timing claim: fetch is one byte per cycle, and cycle counts here say
  nothing about any historical machine;
- the formal core proof is bounded at 12 cycles with covers at 14, memory data
  is free, and core induction is not claimed, so "load returns what was
  stored" is covered only by simulation;
- **no independent oracle yet**: both the testbench expectations and the formal
  reference expressions are a reading of the same manual by the same author.
  Issue #383's external emulator and corpus are required before any
  compatibility claim; #441's diagnostics and #429's BOS/360 boot are required
  before any "runs real S/360 software" claim.
