# 74x762: octal mixed inverting/non-inverting open-collector buffer/driver

**First manufacturer documentation:** December 1983 product preview; commercial introduction year is not established.
**Manufacturer source:** Texas Instruments, [1984 TTL Data Book Volume 3 supplement, SN74AS762 logic symbols](https://archive.org/details/bitsavers_tidataBookVol3_25840031/page/n533). The high-resolution logic-symbol crop is from PDF page index 533 (printed page 2-501). The cached primary PDF is `B:/ai_hw_engineer/.firecrawl/ttl-ti-1984-volume3-primary.pdf`, SHA-256 `DA11F2DEBAF1FF08445E3E73AAAB73A01338F43791CC9C96ED60051E996EF79D`.
**Status:** implemented from the manufacturer's logic symbols.

The SN74AS762 is an octal buffer/driver divided into two independently enabled groups of four. The manufacturer symbol marks both group enables active-low, marks both outputs open-collector, and draws an output inversion triangle only on group 1. Thus, with the group enabled, group 1 outputs the complement of its input and group 2 outputs its input. This source page provides logic symbols, rather than a separate Boolean function table.

| Group | Inputs | Outputs | Enable | Enabled logical output | Output type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1A1–1A4 | 1Y1–1Y4 | 1G low | 1Y = NOT 1A | Open collector |
| 2 | 2A1–2A4 | 2Y1–2Y4 | 2G low | 2Y = 2A | Open collector |

The normalized vector ports use bit 0 for channel 1 through bit 3 for channel 4. `g1_n` and `g2_n` correspond to active-low 1G and 2G. Each `*_drive` flag is high when its bank is enabled. Open-collector output values follow the library convention: zero sinks and one releases to the pull-up. A disabled bank has a low drive flag and its output data is normalized to zero and ignored.

## Interface and limits

The model retains the two independent active-low enables, independent four-bit groups, group 1 inversion, group 2 non-inversion, and open-collector output classification. It does not model voltage, current, propagation delay, hazards, or electrical contention. The digital interface exposes drive state; the surrounding bus model resolves released/sinking outputs. The first-introduction year is unknown; the cited TI preview is evidence of documentation no later than December 1983, not proof of the original commercial launch date.
